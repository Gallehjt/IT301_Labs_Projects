The History of JMU

JMU started as a school for women preparing to become teachers. Over the years, it expanded into the university we know today.

Important Dates

  1. 1908 — JMU was founded, although it was named The State Normal and Industrial School for Women at Harrisonburg, and it functioned as a school for teachers.
  2. 1931 — Wilson Hall was dedicated and became one of the campus's most recognizable buildings. You can hear the clock tower chime every hour.
  3. 1938 — The school was renamed to Madison College.
  4. 1966 — Madison College became fully coeducational, and was desegregated as well.
  5. 1977 — The school was again renamed to James Madison University.

You can still see part of JMU's history in the older buildings around the Quad.
